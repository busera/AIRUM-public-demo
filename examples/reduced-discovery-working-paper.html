How to use this working paper in discovery

  • Start with the scope assumptions and ask the auditee to correct them.
  • Use the candidate AI Risks as discussion prompts, not as final findings.
  • Confirm whether the expected controls exist, who owns them, and what evidence is available.
  • Capture provisional impact and likelihood only as auditee discovery input.
  • Mark each topic as retain, escalate, defer, or remove after the fact pattern is understood.

Discovery rating anchors

This reduced example keeps the 1/3/5/7/9 capture fields to show the workflow, but the ratings are placeholders for discussion. They are not residual risk ratings.

1
Minimal relevance or impact based on confirmed facts.
3
Limited relevance, local effect, or compensating controls likely present.
5
Moderate relevance or unclear control/evidence position.
7
High relevance, material process impact, or weak control visibility.
9
Severe potential impact, broad exposure, or urgent specialist dependency.

1. AI inventory and ownership gaps

Strongly recommendedBaseline governance

Risk description
AI-enabled tools, models, or embedded platform features may be used without a complete inventory, named owner, approved purpose, or clear accountability route. This can leave Finance unable to explain which AI capabilities exist, who is responsible for them, and which governance path applies.
Why this appears
The workflow uses AI-enabled features, but ownership, approval route, model/service inventory, and accountable business owner are not yet confirmed.
Expected controls
AI use inventory, ownership assignment, intake/approval record, periodic review, and clear accountability for business process use.
Discovery questions
Who owns the AI use in Finance? Was it approved through a formal intake path? Is it listed in an AI, model, technology, or vendor inventory?
Evidence to request
Inventory extract, approval record, owner/RACI, operating procedure, governance forum minutes, and policy exception log if applicable.

2. Data quality and lineage weakness

Strongly recommendedData management

Risk description
AI-supported reporting may rely on incomplete, poorly reconciled, or weakly traceable source data. If inputs, transformations, prompts, retrieval sources, and generated outputs are not linked back to reliable records, reviewers may not be able to verify the basis for management reporting conclusions.
Why this appears
The AI-supported reporting workflow depends on source data, transformations, prompts or retrieval context, and generated explanations. Weak lineage could make outputs hard to verify.
Expected controls
Defined source systems, data-quality checks, transformation documentation, ownership of reference data, and documented handling of AI inputs/outputs.
Discovery questions
Which data sources feed the AI-supported process? How are data quality issues detected? Are prompts, uploads, outputs, and retrieval sources retained or reviewed?
Evidence to request
Data lineage diagram, data-quality rules, exception reports, reconciliation evidence, source-system owner confirmation, and retention settings.

3. Human review over AI-generated explanations

Strongly recommendedAccountability

Risk description
AI-generated classifications, summaries, or variance explanations may be accepted without enough human challenge. This can allow plausible but unsupported statements to enter reports, evidence packs, or decision material as if they had been independently verified.
Why this appears
The process may use AI to draft explanations or classify variances. If reviewers accept outputs without challenge, errors can become part of management reporting.
Expected controls
Defined review criteria, reviewer competence, exception handling, evidence of challenge, and clear rules for when AI output cannot be used without additional support.
Discovery questions
Who reviews AI-generated content? What evidence shows that reviewers challenged weak or unsupported outputs? Are there thresholds for manual rework?
Evidence to request
Review checklist, approval sample, rejected-output examples, reviewer training material, and reporting package sign-off evidence.
Retain

4. Vendor or embedded AI dependency

RecommendedThird-party dependency

Risk description
The AI capability may be delivered through a vendor platform, SaaS feature, or external model service rather than a system fully controlled by the organization. Responsibilities for data handling, model changes, logging, incident support, and assurance evidence may therefore sit partly outside Finance.
Why this appears
The AI capability may be embedded in a platform or external service. That creates questions about contractual responsibility, data processing, model changes, logging, and support.
Expected controls
Vendor due diligence, contractual AI/data terms, change notification, access control, monitoring responsibilities, and incident/support routes.
Discovery questions
Is the AI feature provided by a vendor? What data is sent to the service? Does the contract explain model changes, retention, confidentiality, and support responsibilities?
Evidence to request
Contract extract, vendor assurance material, data-processing summary, change log, access review, and service configuration screenshots.

5. Monitoring and change-management gaps

RecommendedLifecycle control

Risk description
AI-supported workflows can change through model updates, vendor releases, prompt revisions, data drift, control changes, or process redesign. Without defined monitoring and change review, the workflow may continue to operate even when outputs, assumptions, or control expectations have shifted.
Why this appears
AI-supported workflows can change through model updates, vendor releases, prompt changes, data drift, or process redesign. Monitoring may be unclear before discovery.
Expected controls
Change log, release review, monitoring indicators, owner review cadence, incident route, and documented response to degraded or unexpected outputs.
Discovery questions
How are changes to the AI feature or its inputs reviewed? What monitoring shows that outputs remain reliable? What happens when the AI produces a poor explanation?
Evidence to request
Change records, monitoring dashboard, issue log, release notes, exception handling procedure, and periodic control review evidence.

Public boundary

This example shows the shape of an AIRUM output, not the complete method. A real AIRUM export may include more candidate AI Risks, richer rationale, source basis, applicability prompts, and evidence-pack details. Those details are intentionally reduced here.

AIRUM is a preparation aid. It does not decide audit scope, assess control effectiveness, calculate residual risk, provide legal or compliance advice, or deliver audit assurance.
